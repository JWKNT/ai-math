Any flow of fluid solves the equations for some force. Take the flow, put it into the equations, and call whatever is left over the force. So the real task is to choose a flow that blows up while that left-over force stays smooth, even at the moment of blow-up. The single terms of the equations may become infinite. Their sum, and every rate of change of the sum, must stay finite and smooth.

The flow is a slender vortex around one point, in which fluid spirals inward toward the axis. Then it flows out along the axis, up on one side of a middle layer and down on the other. The inward motion spins the vortex up. A ring of fluid that moves from radius 2 to radius 1, with no twisting force on it, doubles its speed around the axis. A skater pulls in their arms and spins faster in the same way. The flow out along the axis removes the incoming fluid, so the inflow can continue.

To first order the vortex keeps its shape and only changes its scale. Write \(\tau\) for the time left before blow-up. The radius of the core shrinks like \(\sqrt{\tau}\), and its height shrinks a little more slowly, so the core becomes a thinner and thinner column. When \(\tau\) falls from 0.01 to 0.0001, the radius falls 10 times, and the speeds grow more than 10 times. The energy of the core still falls to 0, because the core becomes so small.

The difficult part is the edge of the core. Joining the core to calm fluid outside leaves an imbalance in a ring around it, and that imbalance would need an infinite force. The proof cancels it with many small pulses that oscillate quickly in space. The push of the pulses on one another cancels the infinite part of the imbalance. The shear of the spinning core makes the pulses grow from a tiny starting force.

A perfectly symmetric core would fail at its middle layer, where the shear would vanish. So the paper gives the core a slight upward bias. Then further corrections, one after another, remove every remaining error. What is left is a force that stays smooth through the moment of blow-up. It is also zero outside a fixed region of space and time.

The fluid starts at rest, its total energy stays limited, and its speed becomes infinite at time 1. This settles the case with a force. The case with no force is a different question, and it stays open. OpenAI reports this proof, with a version in Lean, a computer program that checks each step of a proof. A later paper argues that this Lean version does not match the written proof.
