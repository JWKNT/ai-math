Non-Sofic Groups

What it saysHow the proof works

The proof needs a group whose table cannot be imitated by shuffles of a finite list, even with small errors. A group is a set of actions that you can combine and undo. The proof combines two known results about shuffles with one new step. Then it finds a group in which all three apply together.

The first known result concerns groups with property (T), a strong form of rigidity. Gábor Kun proved that shuffles that imitate such a group must link the positions into well-connected pieces, called expanders. In an expander, every set of at most half the positions has many links to the rest, in proportion to its size. A clock is a poor expander. On a clock with 12 positions, the 6 positions from 0 to 5 have only 2 links to the other 6.

The second known result is by Kun and Andreas Thom. Suppose a group with property (T) and a second group J commute, and the shuffles form one single expander. Then every finite part of the table of J must fit exactly inside a finite group. But imitations can split into many expanders, and then a commuting group can move between the pieces. So the second result does not apply directly.

The new step closes that gap. It adds actions that move the rigid group, and J, inside the rigid group itself. The proof then measures the size of the expander piece that holds each position. These actions can only move positions toward pieces that are at least as large, and a shuffle has to undo every move it makes. So the sizes change very little, and in effect the imitation behaves like one expander. Then J must fit inside finite groups after all.

The last task is an example where all of this holds and J fails that test. The paper uses tables of operations on endless strings of 0s and 1s, which come from the binary Leavitt algebra. These tables have property (T), by a theorem of Mikhail Ershov and Andrei Jaikin-Zapirain. For J it uses Thompson's group V, which rearranges the same strings by their starting digits.

Group V is infinite, it is fixed by a finite list of rules, and it has no proper parts that collapse it. Such a group cannot fit its finite tables inside finite groups. So the big group cannot be imitated by shuffles, and it is not sofic. OpenAI reports this proof. Francesco Fournier-Facio later built other examples, with no repeating actions, from the same new step.
